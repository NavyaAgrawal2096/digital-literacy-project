## Understanding UPI and Online Payment Fraud

UPI and online payment fraud is a sophisticated type of cybercrime in which criminals strategically trick individuals into sending money or sharing confidential banking credentials. College students are frequently targeted by these syndicates because they rely heavily on UPI apps for daily activities like ordering food, shopping, commuting, and splitting bills with friends.

A common fraud cycle typically begins when a victim receives a deceptive phone call, an urgent SMS, or a fake "money received" notification. Scammers often use social engineering to pretend to be bank employees, delivery agents, or online sellers. They create a sense of false urgency, pressured the victim to click on a suspicious link or "authorize" a payment request within their UPI app.

A dangerous and widespread misconception is the belief that entering a PIN or accepting a request is required to receive money. In reality, your UPI PIN is only required to send money, never to receive it. Once a victim shares an OTP, UPI PIN, or card details, the funds are instantly siphoned into untraceable accounts.

The consequences go beyond mere financial loss; they include significant emotional stress and the risk of identity theft. In many cases, recovered funds are rare once the transaction is authorized. To stay secure, users must remain vigilant: never share an OTP, ignore unsolicited payment requests, and always verify the recipient's identity before hitting "pay."
